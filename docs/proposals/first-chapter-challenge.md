# First-chapter production challenge

Status: proposal for `factory-yau.1.1`, 2026-10-14. No product, material, recipe,
count, part, unlock, or chapter order below is accepted game content. The counts
make the paper example concrete; they are not throughput or performance targets.
[DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) remains authoritative. This draft supplies the
challenge for layout, walkthrough, resource, and independent-review tasks; it is
not a finished layout, playtest result, or first-release commitment.

## What the chapter should establish

The player makes something useful with a small mechanical vocabulary, automates
the handling they initially perform by hand, learns a process that the first
method cannot perform, then chooses whether to expand or redesign earlier
production. The chapter should leave a factory worth watching, rather than a
sequence of hand-ins or three complete machines placed beside one another.

The proposed objective is to prepare freight-frame kits for an off-site
commission. The manager can explain that practical objective without revealing
the megaproject. A kit is a packed set of components, not an instant factory or
a selected construction blueprint. Its exact use and narrative remain open.

## Candidate 1: pressing and rolling work metal

Use a fictional, naturally workable material called **softmetal** so this
candidate can investigate forming before adding a furnace or chemical chain.
That material premise needs a user choice; it is not a claim about real ore.
A finite exposed seam yields standardized blanks when cut. Initially the player
cuts and carries blanks with a hand tool; a complete powered cutting head is a
candidate way to automate the same finite source later.

### Concrete A/B/C relationship

| Role | Provisional product and inputs | Process and player problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Construction plate: 1 softmetal blank → 1 plate | Place a blank beneath a complete drop-press head, arrange its drive and clearance, then remove the plate. Replace those handling steps with an arranged feed/carrier/discharge path. |
| B | Guide channel: 1 softmetal blank → 1 channel | Feed through an opposed pair of forming rolls. The flat press cannot produce the channel's continuous folded section. The player must arrange counterrotation, feed direction, support and exit clearance. |
| C | Freight-frame kit: 12 plates + 2 guide channels → 1 kit | Physically collect and pack both products. Several kits raise demand for A after the player has learned rolling for B. Packing is a provisional inventory/workbench action, not a new processing lesson or proof of automation. |
| Alternative A route | 1 softmetal blank → 1 plate through flat rolls | Adapt the newly learned opposed-roll arrangement with flat tooling. It makes the same useful A, with a different feed/power/clearance problem rather than a larger output multiplier on the press. |

An illustrative first check asks for 4 plates; a later commission asks for
3 kits, containing 36 plates and 6 channels. Those quantities create a visible
increase in A demand without prescribing a timer, required rate, or sustained
automation check. They need balancing against construction costs and deposit
size. Checking an item and physically shipping it are separate unresolved
operations; this draft does not decide whether checks consume goods.

The recipes account for one blank per formed part. They provisionally omit
scrap, fuel and tool wear rather than hiding them as recurring chores. If the
chosen material/process needs any of those inputs or outputs, the resource
proposal must include their handling and eventual automation before this chain
can be accepted. Kit unpacking, recovery and packing automation also need a
defined eventual route; a hand-only packing step cannot remain a permanent
exception to the automation goal.

The new-process distinction must follow the chosen tools and material behavior.
If an inventive arrangement of the old flat heads can already form B, allow that
valid construction and revise B or the learning sequence. A recipe tag forbidding
the old machine would not establish a genuinely new capability.

### Small construction vocabulary

| Part family | Proposed responsibility | What the player still constructs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanical power input | A bounded starter source supplies rotation; its final type is open | Shaft route, gearing, direction changes and branch isolation |
| Shafts, gears, clutch/slipping coupling | Transmit rotation, set relative direction/cycle relationships, isolate a branch | The actual transmission topology, rather than a universal power cable |
| Drop-press head and passive anvil | A complete functional head lifts/releases a ram on a predictable repeating cycle | Working height, supports, feed alignment and moving carrier beneath it |
| Belts, chutes, guide stops and pocketed carriers | Move/support discrete blanks and products | Physical supply and discharge paths, with useful placement constraints |
| Rotary carrier or reversing gantry | A fixed repeating movement; gantry reverses at end stops | How that movement presents work beneath tools and separates feed from output |
| Opposed roll heads with flat/channel tooling | Form the two output sections from aligned blanks | Paired shafts, counterrotation, feed support and changing the tooling while stationary |
| Powered source cutter, if included | Cuts blanks from the same depleting seam | Movement, reach and material collection; it does not make the seam infinite |

These are proposed functional responsibilities, not selected block dimensions,
interfaces, recipes for every part, or an assumed rigid-body simulation.
Rotation and gantries are alternative early motion arrangements, not an agreed
technology order. Forming rolls add a process; they do not establish that all
rotary motion must be locked behind B.

No sensor, counter, programmable sequencer or configurable cycle timer is needed
to complete the proposed chapter. A pocket admits one blank by its shape;
placement, fixed motion and gearing coordinate handling. Whether that compact
vocabulary really supports unattended feed and discharge is an explicit layout
question, not an already demonstrated result.

Walkthroughs must preserve the accepted connected-drivetrain stall without
damage and branch isolation. Distinguishing intended forming contact from an
obstruction belongs with the mechanism fixtures; this draft selects no detailed
collision response, clearance warning or activation restriction.

### Proposed learning and unlock sequence

1. **Make A manually.** The player cuts a few blanks, powers an accessible press
   and places/removes work by hand. A visual explanation teaches the tool face,
   motion and gearing rule. The first item check confirms a product, not a claim
   that it was automated.
2. **Remove press tending.** Offer a related optional project to carry blanks
   through pressing and discharge them. A rotary carrier and a gantry are both
   candidates. Completing the chapter must not require copying one layout.
3. **Introduce B through rolling.** A small A milestone can provisionally expose
   roll tooling and its explanation. Roll construction uses available A and
   starter/common parts, never B or C; the supply task must check that bootstrap.
   The new guide-channel product gives a reason to learn the process even if
   the original A line is satisfactory.
4. **Ask for C.** The kit commission increases A demand. Keep expanding the press
   route viable; offer adapting rolls for plates as the redesign opportunity.
   Neither route is invalidated by a technology-tier prohibition.
5. **Open a supply project through capability.** A candidate milestone is making
   the first B, independently of remaining seam reserves. It can expose a
   lasting-supply project while C remains approachable. The chapter's actual
   milestone, source route and pacing belong to `factory-yau.2.5` and the user
   decision packet, not this proposed sequence.

The starting fixture needs enough parts to build and dismantle one small
arrangement. A repeatable fixture stock is not a world rule or unlimited player
inventory. Construction-part replenishment and all prerequisites must be checked
before calling this a viable campaign start.

### Scaling and three-dimensional questions to test

The initial press is compact in plan but needs genuine working height for the
descending ram, its lifting mechanism and the carrier beneath it. Gravity feed
and discharge can use levels above and below that working plane. Height must
affect operation and access, not merely satisfy an arbitrary minimum-height
recipe rule. A head with hidden height inside a prefab would not test the goal.

The layout task should compare a rotary carrier's swept area with a fixed-stroke
gantry arrangement that adds working lanes perpendicular to travel. Both still
need enough heads, feed paths and transmission. A gantry is not inherently better:
its carrier, return path, reach and supported loads may make it worse for a small
batch. These sketches need real layouts before anyone calls them workable.

The roll route's proposed reward is continuous feed without repeatedly moving
work into and out of a pressing stroke, and potentially less height per line.
Its costs are a longer supported path, two coordinated drive branches, exit
clearance and more careful material orientation. Compare equivalent feedstock
and output, including construction effort and handling. Do not assume a speed,
power, yield or capacity bonus merely because the tooling unlocks later.

Expanded presses remain useful for small or interrupted batches, constrained
sites, existing installations and mixed production. Multiple roll lanes and
multiple presses are honest duplication controls. The advanced route earns its
place only if its different arrangement offers a meaningful advantage after
accounting for those costs. If it does not, revise the challenge rather than
penalizing the old recipe.

## Candidate 2: shaving and turning wood

This alternative uses familiar feedstock and makes workpiece motion central,
but introduces more workholding and preparation questions.

| Role | Provisional relationship |
| --- | --- |
| Input | Straight-grained timber billets, cut from finite accessible timber; source replenishment/automation remains a separate design obligation |
| A | 1 billet → 1 wooden shaft, pushed through a complete shaving head on a fixed reciprocating carrier |
| B | 1 disk blank → 1 bored hub, requiring a boring head and a rotating, held workpiece; a saw produces disk blanks from the same timber feedstock |
| C | 12 shafts + 2 bored hubs → 1 transmission kit, provisionally packed as for Candidate 1 |
| Adapt the new process for A | Turn the same billet into a shaft using the rotating workholding arrangement and an external cutting head instead of the boring head |

The proposed new capability is controlled workpiece machining with a tool feed,
not the invention of rotation itself. Basic rotary carriers and gantries remain
available for earlier arrangements. Candidate unlocks mirror the product
relationship: make shafts, learn boring for hubs, then consider turning shafts
when kits increase demand. They do not establish a global technology order.

This needs complete saw, shaving, boring and external-cutting heads, a chuck or
equivalent workholder, a mechanically fed slide, fixed-cycle travel, shafts/gears
and chip collection. The player initially cuts/carries billets and tends the
shaving operation. A turning line might handle longer stock or share automated
clamping/feed across products, while a short shaving line might stay cheaper
and easier to arrange. Those advantages require testing, especially whether
turning offers enough benefit to justify rebuilding A.

Reject or revise this candidate if clamping/releasing requires advanced control,
chips create unautomatable maintenance, stock preparation hides another large
chain, or verticality is only decorative. Its weaker ordinary reason for height
and larger tool vocabulary are reasons to investigate Candidate 1 first, not
evidence that Candidate 1 has already won.

## Interfaces, evidence and decisions

The layout task (`factory-yau.1.2`) owns concrete alternative arrangements and
comparative costs. The walkthrough task (`factory-yau.1.3`) owns build/diagnose/
recover/expand and the teaching storyboard. The resource task
(`factory-yau.2.5`) owns source lifecycles, exploration shortcuts versus harder
in-base routes, construction bootstrap, and avoiding exhausted prerequisites.
All should be free to reject these provisional recipes.

The ownership packet (`factory-yau.2.2`/`.2.3`) must decide how product checks,
shared production credit and off-site shipping relate. Construction-access
options (`factory-yau.2.4`) determine how the player retrieves parts; this draft
assumes physical item movement and grants no remote inventory withdrawal.

At proposal review, ask:

1. Is the softmetal premise acceptable, or is familiar timber worth the larger
   preparation/workholding vocabulary? Another material/process combination is
   still possible.
2. Does the press/roll contrast sound like a satisfying change in engineering,
   and which benefit should make adapting the new process worth doing: handling,
   working space, capacity, or another concrete axis?
3. Is a component kit an appropriate first commission, or should C instead be
   a functional assembled product with its own explicitly scoped assembly rule?
4. Which parts and fixed-cycle behaviors are needed from the start? In
   particular, can feed, discharge and recovery work without early general
   controls or routine tending?
5. Do the proposed product milestones and the separately designed supply project
   give players a route past initial extraction chores without depending on a
   node exhausting? Which rules are ready for the first-playable decision and
   which should remain open?

Before acceptance, reviewers should trace named inputs, bootstrap stock, kit
handling and byproducts through the whole route; compare two materially
different layouts; and reject any new input that quietly restores mandatory
manual upkeep. Ordinary item confirmation must stay distinct from automation
evidence and the later true-ending throughput objective. No playability, fun,
continuous operation or numerical advantage has been demonstrated by this draft.

This proposal leaves engine/runtime choice, block dimensions, final camera,
numerical performance/network budgets, detailed contact/injury/docking rules and
independent machine pushing untouched. Broader moving factories, passengers,
trains, later controls, fluids/gases and campaign endings remain intended-game
requirements, not omissions from the plan.
