# Chapter supply: finite extraction to lasting production

Status: paper proposal for `factory-yau.2.5`, 2026-10-14. All new materials,
sources, recipes, parts, counts and safeguards below need user review;
[DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) remains authoritative. No runtime or supply balance
has been tested. This develops the softmetal candidate in
[first-chapter-challenge.md](first-chapter-challenge.md), not a generic resource
catalogue. The alternative timber chapter needs its own planting/workholding/
chip-accounting proposal; it does not inherit this proof.

## Proposed route and the chapter decision

Keep the draft relationship: one blank makes one plate A or guide channel B;
12 A + 2 B make kit C. Presses and flat rolls remain valid A routes. Start with
a finite exposed seam: hand cutting removes a blank permanently; a powered
cutter later automates extraction and collection from the same diminishing node.
A full receiver stops extraction before reserving/removing another blank.
Neither unloading the area nor waiting restores the seam. Moving to a second
designed, reachable seam is an explicit simpler option, with physical haulage.

After initial seam cutting is established and the first B is made and confirmed,
offer the supply project regardless of seam reserves. Record the first extraction
as a prerequisite event, not an exhaustion condition. Making B is a capability
milestone, not proof of automation.
The check must preserve the bootstrap B or provide an equivalent reserve if
the eventual consumption contract differs. Exhausting a seam before B grants
no technology; making B while the seam is almost untouched still opens supply.

Propose a **deep softmetal flow formation**: fresh workable metal arrives from
outside the locally mined volume. A tap extracts ribbon which a reusable cutter
divides into standard blanks. This is a fictional infinite-source premise, not
real geology, passive regeneration of deposits, or a pile-size threshold. It
adds no fuel, chemistry, scrap or refining chain to the first chapter.

| Same capability, two routes | Construction and physical path | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exploration shortcut | A designed accessible fissure exposes the flow; a proposed tap costs 2 A + 1 B plus reusable common fittings. Ribbon → cutter → local bin → belt/cart to base | Less source machinery, but a longer transport/power route; no rare random find or unique consumable key |
| Harder in-base route | At the starting site's designated deep access point, a proposed bore/tap costs 4 A + 2 B plus the same fittings. Guide the boring assembly, install casing, connect drive, then route ribbon through cutter to base feed | More construction, vertical access and shaft routing; avoids an expedition and its long haul |

The bore is a scoped source operation, not permission for early general terrain
reshaping. Installation removes one finite rock plug into a local receiver;
retain it as a foundation block, with room reserved before drilling. There is
no continuing rock waste once the tap reaches the flow. Its module includes
reusable bit/casing; it needs no sacrificial bit, coolant or hidden B-consuming
maintenance. Startup is hand-powered; a proposed
waterwheel on the designed permanent watercourse later drives extraction,
cutting and transport mechanically. Water supplies motion without becoming a
recipe ingredient; wheel/transport construction uses post-tap A/B and reusable
starter tooling, not another finite-only feedstock. Exact power/load, source
geometry and qualifying conditions need decisions; accumulating metal in a
chest cannot create a flow formation.

The shortcut is optional. No resource, map token or technology obtained only
there is required to construct the base route. Both remain locked until the
capability milestone, and both feed existing press/roll lines rather than
granting instant finished kits. Reject this premise if its fictional source or
extra boring vocabulary is unsuitable; do not quietly substitute replenishing
early seams or a mandatory fluid-processing chapter.

## Inputs, outputs and eventual unattended paths

The following is the complete proposed material boundary. Introducing another
consumable or waste stream invalidates it until that stream has its own source,
transport, recovery and eventual unattended handling.

| Input or stock | Output/byproduct and handling | Recovery and ultimate operation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finite seam metal | One removed blank enters cutter receiver; no scrap in this candidate | Retained blank survives a stall; receiver backpressure prevents loss. Later mobile acquisition can automate new finite sites |
| Flow ribbon | Cutter produces equivalent blank quantities; partial ribbon remains in-process, no discard | Tap pauses when downstream is full; resumes through the same physical cutter/receiver, not periodic refill |
| Rock at the designated base access point | One installation plug → reusable foundation block; no ore-processing residue | Physical receiver retains it; powered handling can move it to local construction storage. Full receiver prevents drilling |
| Blank | Press or flat rolls → A; profile rolls → B; no fuel, chips or tool wear proposed | Feed/carrier/discharge follow layout dwell and occupancy rules; invalid input stalls for manual or designed correction |
| 12 A + 2 B | One C, provisionally a component bundle with no disposable wrapping | Start with local bench packing; proposed later fixed-pocket bundler loads 12+2, fastens without consumable binder, discharges C. Reusable nests/tools return each cycle |
| C or recoverable packed work | Unpacking returns the same 12 A + 2 B; half-filled bundles retain actual contents | Later mechanical unpack/return path makes recovery automatable; a completed delivery removes goods only under the accepted shipping contract |
| Starter fittings, complete heads, drive and transport parts | Installed durable machinery, no hidden lubricant/replacement stock | Proposed local assembly from A/B can replenish parts using reusable starter tooling; later powered assembly automates it. Dismantling returns parts without loss |
| Watercourse motion / hand input | Shaft motion, no material output or fuel waste | Waterwheel replaces manual cranking; hand input is bootstrap, not the lasting operating plan |

Bundling and part assembly are explicit proposed follow-on mechanisms, not
already working interfaces. Their concrete head/part bills and automatic feed/
unpack paths must be validated before claiming whole-chain unattended operation.
No one-off starter fitting may become an irreplaceable consumed catalyst.
Ordinary inventory confirmation, physical shipment, consumption and credit
remain separate decisions; no sustained-rate quest check is added here.

Every arrow means local handling: seam/tap → receiver → physical belt or cart
→ local buffer → press/roll → A/B bins → packing → dispatch. Return paths carry
misfeeds/unpacked parts to the appropriate buffer. No catalogue grants remote
withdrawal. A full output blocks upstream work without deleting items; isolated
drive branches let a powered takeaway clear recoverable backpressure. Solid
obstruction removal may initially be manual. Automatic recovery must exercise
actual retained phase/contents; the reviewed layouts/walkthrough expose that
unproven dependency. No general early programmable controller is assumed.
Normal solved operation must not need periodic manual jam clearing; deliberate
fault injection is different from a design that regularly jams on its own.

## Bootstrap and depletion argument

Proposed minimal protected stock: reusable starter press, roll tooling, common
fittings/cutter and hand drive, plus **six blanks**. Four become A and two B,
enough for the costlier base tap; the first B also establishes the milestone.
Initial press/roll construction uses the durable kit, not B or C. The source
rig does not depend on its own output. A local parts bench reuses that tooling
to make additional construction stock once the tap supplies blanks.
All prerequisite capability checks must preserve protected stock, including
the initial A check. If checks consume goods, increase the reserve by their
full prerequisite bill before acceptance; six alone would then be insufficient.

This needs an explicit **recoverable construction reserve** rule: the physical
starter kit and those six blank-equivalents may become bootstrap A/B or rig
parts but cannot be irreversibly shipped, discarded or destroyed. Dismantling
the rig returns its invested A/B. The reserve stays at actual item locations;
it is not a respawn grant or remote withdrawal. How custody and permitted use
work in a shared world belongs to the ownership decision, not an assumed team
lock here. This safeguard restricts spending and needs explicit user approval.

Under that proposed invariant, even after every ordinary seam is exhausted,
the retained kit and six equivalents can establish/rebuild the base tap after
the B milestone. This assumes initial extraction was established; late arrivals
to an already depleted shared world require the ownership/access decision.
Before B, the reserve can still produce B and establish that milestone. After
tap startup, ribbon supplies fresh blanks and hence A/B, replacement machinery
and C. Water motion removes manual power input. None of those arrows requires
an exhausted seam, a unique exploration item, consumed tooling or shipped C.
The illustrative three kits need 42 further blanks; output checks, construction
and initial four-plate demand must be budgeted separately, never borrowed from
the protected six. These are paper counts, not selected deposit sizes.

Without reserve protection or an equivalent resource-independent rebuild rule,
this is **not** an exhaustion-proof design: more finite nodes merely postpone
the same failure. If the user rejects the safeguard, revise bootstrap/access
before accepting the chapter. Shared-world theft/access, loss of reusable tools,
terrain blocking and alternative quest-consumption rules must be tested against
the invariant; this proposal does not establish their resolved semantics.

## Preserve the finite route and test the claims

Repeat-node acquisition remains useful for easy nearby stock and avoiding the
tap project. Later autonomous finite acquisition should survey the next deposit,
move a harvesting assembly, cut/collect while moving, transfer to physical
haulage, and move on after depletion without a player placing each replacement
node or rail. Power, route/access changes, empty-site detection and unload
backpressure are part of that later requirement, not solved by a stationary
powered cutter. It complements infinite sources in the intended game; it is
not required in the proposed first-chapter demo. A finite designed world does
not become mathematically infinite through autonomous surveying. Active
recovery-cycle sources remain later scope; passive waiting is no substitute.

Future acceptance runs should (1) exhaust one seam and inspect unchanged empty
state after reload; (2) work a second seam and haul its stock; (3) reach B with
high and zero ordinary reserves and get the same unlock; (4) spend/ship all
unprotected goods and rebuild the base route from the reserve alone; (5) build
the base route without visiting the shortcut and compare its real effort with
the shortcut; (6) trace input, in-flight work, output and unpacked returns through
full receivers, stalls and restart. Attempt forbidden reserve spending and
shared-world loss as negative cases. All runs are currently unperformed.

For the decision packet, choose or reject: the flow-source premise and two
access routes; B-based availability; six-equivalent recoverable reserve/custody;
and no-wear/no-scrap, binder-free packing and reusable part tooling. Review the
actual construction bills and automatic recovery before those become playable
contracts. Reserve/ownership and whole-chain automation remain acceptance risks,
not evidence that an installed source alone delivers an unattended chapter.
