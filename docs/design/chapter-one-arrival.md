# Chapter one: arrival and the first workshop

Status: user-directed chapter direction, revised 2026-10-24. The powered-slide,
interchangeable-working-head and passive-workbed boundary below is the approved
plan of record. The landing pod, manual
harvesting, locally made components, modular collector and modular press replace
the old complete-machine opening as the target experience. Names, quantities and
recipes here are reversible prototype choices until tested in a running game.
[DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) owns the wider requirements. Existing collector,
press and four-plate fixtures remain useful regression evidence, not an opening
that new players must follow.

## The player's first ten minutes

The player is a field fabricator contracted by the **Meridian Combine**, an
industrial corporation whose operations manager is an AI. The Combine has sent
workers to an apparently peaceful alien frontier to ship manufactured components
for an off-site megaproject. The first work order is specific; its final purpose
is withheld. This premise supports dry humor and later discovery without making
dialogue, employment obedience or a single factory layout a construction gate.

A descent view establishes the forest, stream and ore outcrops. The player wakes
in a work suit with a visible multitool. The pod settles
on the same world grid as player construction, then unfolds into a small, usable
foundation deck, workbench, storage tray and detachable hand crank. The hatch
opens onto the site. The pod is the first example of a compact machine unfolding
into a useful arrangement, not a magical source of unlimited stock. The player
can inspect it, build beside it and eventually replace its functions and reclaim
its parts. The scene should be skippable and rejoin safely at its settled state.

The first workshop task completes when the player has one formed plate in their
own inventory. There is no shipment destination in the opening worksite. Outside
the supplied pod/workshop, the player builds the visible machinery.

The AI offers one instruction at a time, with replay in the work log. Candidate
lines are short enough to leave the game view readable:

> “Landfall confirmed. Your office is now the part of the planet under your boots.”

> “The kit makes tools. The tools make a factory. Corporate has provided the kit.”

> “Form one plate from local stock and keep it in your inventory.”

The player can cut nearby timber or ore first, make simple parts at the hub or
with the multitool, and choose the location of the first machine. A suggested
route teaches a hand-tended press and a hand-cranked collector, then shows how
the same functions can be driven and fed automatically. Those are examples, not
an exact blueprint or a tech lock. The first task asks for one carried formed
plate. Later optional projects can make unattended collection and feed valuable.
This leaves time to design a collector without making a fixed assembly sequence
the only way to progress.

## Physical starting state

| Thing | Initial state and role |
| --- | --- |
| Player | One physical actor in a work suit, with a reusable multitool. Actor position limits harvesting, hub use and construction. The camera alone has no reach authority. |
| Pod | One deployed site object per landing. Its deck uses the global foundation origin and height denominator, with ordinary vertical and horizontal joins. Its hub and tray have explicit identities and stock custody. A late join never creates another free pod or kit. |
| Hub | A local clamp/cutting/forming surface for component work. It has modest carried-item input and output capacity, and can be replaced by a player-built bench. Basic wood shaping remains possible with the multitool away from the pod; metal forming needs a bench or an equivalent assembled fixture. |
| Hand crank | One detachable, reusable item. It supplies rotation only while an actor turns it at a shaft; it can be moved between early machines. The eventual waterwheel can run them without a player. No hidden motor, battery or endless pod power is implied. |
| Site | At least three reachable, visibly harvestable trees and one finite softmetal ore deposit near the pod, plus a stream and room for different layouts. These are world stocks rather than inventory grants. The pod is not a restricted build rectangle. |

Multitool cutting reduces a specific tree or deposit's finite stock and creates
carried items at that actor. Sawing timber and cutting a softmetal blank take
time and show contact/collection feedback. Refused range, wrong target,
source depletion and repeated command cannot duplicate or erase material. The
opening actor can carry its finite material supply without a small hidden bag
limit; a future weight system requires its own design decision. A tree
and ore outcrop remain visibly depleted after save and rejoin. The multitool
also reclaims construction components; occupied machines require recovery of
their actual retained work before an edit that would delete it.

The exact pod footprint and biome are art/layout choices. A 3 × 3 tile deck is
the first presentation target, not a special construction size or final pod
scale. Place it on a designed safe landing surface while letting the player
expand or build elsewhere on the shared world grid. If the terrain changes, the
same deployment transform must place both the visible panels and authoritative
foundations; avoid a visual-only floor or a second mutable pod model.

## Bootstrap materials and component bill

These are **reversible balance inputs** for a finite bootstrap. The approved
powered-slide recipe needs a new complete budget and ordinary construction
proof; the old separate-guide bill does not establish it. One tree yields
eight usable timber pieces; the original
balance fixture used sixteen softmetal blanks. At least three accessible trees
give layout and mistake margin. The playable native arrival now uses a finite
4,096-unit ore reserve; the old sixteen-blank fixture is not its current source
capacity. A different final yield
is fine if the same dependency and recovery proof is redone. Softmetal is a
fictional workable material for early cold forming; its geology and final name
remain open.

| Component | Local fabrication input | Uses in the first arrangements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wooden frame | 3 timber | A load-bearing base for the collector. |
| Wooden shaft | 1 timber | External routing from crank or waterwheel; also the embodied shaft cost in a wooden driven component. |
| Wooden cog | 2 timber | Change rotation direction or drive ratio within its stress rating. |
| Receiver tray | 2 timber | Catches a cut blank; full capacity pauses extraction. |
| Tool mount | 1 timber | Supports the collector head at a chosen height and orientation. |
| Powered slide | Mechanism/body cost plus one same-material shaft; total to be tuned | Complete rotary input, linear motion mechanism, rails and moving carriage. |
| Cutting edge | 1 softmetal blank, subject to width/material tuning | Interchangeable cutting head for a compatible slide or cutter mount. |
| Anvil/workbed | 2 softmetal blanks | Passive support for forming or cutting work. |
| Flat working face | 2 softmetal blanks, subject to area/material tuning | Interchangeable pressing head carried by the slide. |
| Extra foundation | 2 timber | Extends a floor or access step on the shared world grid. |

The first forming arrangement uses a powered slide, a flat working face and
an anvil/workbed, with a detachable crank or an external power route. The input
shaft is charged once in the slide recipe and remains part of that component
through carrying, placement and reclaim; reclaim must not mint a second shaft.
Any rotary-driven collector/tool component follows the same shaft-cost rule.
Loose shafts and cogs are additional routing or gearing choices, not compulsory
local parts used only to satisfy a machine recognizer. Numerical body/mechanism
costs and complete collector-plus-forming bills must be checked against actual
reachable timber and ore before claiming the new bootstrap works.

The current landing carries fifteen spare foundation parts after its nine-panel
pod deck. Three more foundations cost six timber from the same finite trees.
Budget six timber for those three floors and, at current provisional prices,
four timber plus two blanks for one belt and one waterwheel, in addition to the
chosen powered-slide, head, workbed and collector costs. An upper/lower arrangement
can spend four elevated floor panels, four access steps and three routing panels; an
alternative staggered ground arrangement can spend twelve panels. Both fit
within the fifteen supplied spares, while three locally crafted panels extend
either arrangement without a hidden refill. These counts are a material budget,
not a proof that a particular physical route has been built or played.

The collector's frame and mount, the powered slide, working head, workbed,
external transmission and receivers retain their own world identities.
They have placement faces and physical clearance; a recipe creates a component,
not an instant collector or press. The first plate can be made from a blank
cut by hand. The collector reduces manual mining thereafter. Dismantling
returns components rather than a generic machine kit, subject to explicit
retained-item recovery. The player can use other valid arrangements and more
parts; no task requires an exact bill or punishes experimentation by destroying
the finite starter stock.

The pod bench can cold-shape these simple metal parts using blanks the player
has actually harvested. Better formed metal shafts and cogs provisionally cost
two blanks each, so higher quality is available before a production line is
perfect. Later alloys and hardened heads require a new process, not a coloured
copy of the old part. Early wood remains cheap and useful for low-load branches.
The stream is a route to lasting power. Short gravity chutes or wooden rollers
can handle early material; powered belts and future fiber/strip stock need their
own conserved recipes before this prototype's free belt kit is retired. No
single finite tree or deposit is silently made infinite to rescue the fixture.

## Plan of record: powered slides and working heads

The user approved this boundary on 2026-10-24. It replaces the fixed local
guide/ram-body/shaft/cog recipe; it is not a claim that the current runtime
already supplies a reusable motion component.

| Component | Function and choices |
| --- | --- |
| Powered slide | Contains the same-material input shaft, rotary-to-linear mechanism, rails and carriage. Supplies travel axis, stroke, swept clearance and force capacity. Local bearings and cams are not extra assembly chores. |
| Working head | Attaches to the carriage and supplies contact shape, area and tool material. A flat pressing face and a cutting edge are the first required interchangeable uses. |
| Anvil/workbed | Supports work for pressing or cutting. It has no rotary input and does not require a press to be assembled above it. |

The same motion component must be usable for both pressing and cutting. It can
cycle unloaded, without a head, workbed or recognized processing recipe, when
the travel is clear. Stock changes only through actual compatible tool contact,
support, sufficient force/work and available output custody. Larger working
areas increase real load and clearance demand; a stronger head cannot bypass a
weak slide or transmission. Workpiece area and motion stroke are distinct choices.

Keep a simple fixed cycle initially. A pusher or more expressive motion is a
later extension. Structural anchoring remains deferred for the early slide/head;
the workpiece still needs support. All construction sizes and positions use
the shared world grid and height denominator.

A component has the same chosen shape, dimensions and physical envelope in its
preview, alone and connected. Its carriage moves inside the declared sweep under
native power; recognizing a nearby machine never grows rails or extra structure.
Any explicit size or stroke setting is visible before placement and obeys native
clearance. Component inspection describes ports, working area, motion and actual
local refusals. An isolated anvil does not show a missing-part press checklist.
Optional examples teach possible arrangements while preserving other uses.

Implementation acceptance requires the same slide driving both head types,
unloaded cycling, different valid layouts, finite crafting/reclaim, blocked-work
recovery and save/shared continuation in ordinary controls. Inspect preview,
standalone and operating views. Existing fixed-offset press/shear tests remain
evidence for their original implementation, not proof of this reusable contract.
Exact numerical recipes, dimensions and force ratings remain tunable.

The implementation is tracked in `factory-yau.1.10.24`, with stable geometry in
`factory-yau.1.10.8.15` and component inspection in `factory-yau.6.41.54`. All are
owned by the Chapter 1 subtree `factory-yau.1.10`.

## Machinery is an arrangement

The collector needs a frame that supports a cutting head at the deposit face,
a path for the blade to reach exposed stock, a receiver positioned at its
discharge, and a physical drive. Tool orientation and clearance should decide
whether it cuts; an internal “collector present” flag should not. A cutter
driven by the detachable crank can share the same finite deposit and output
port that a later waterwheel route uses. A full tray or belt output stops the
next cut without debiting ore.

The early pressing arrangement brings a flat working head on a powered slide
into contact with stock supported by an anvil/workbed, with room to insert and
remove work. The slide and head may stand in empty space above the workbed;
neither a structural frame nor a mounting point is required at this stage.
A manually placed blank can become a plate
before belt feed is built. Different working heights, shaft routes, feed faces
and gravity paths are valid when contact and clearance conditions hold. The
same native custody owner retains partial work, input and output through a stall, edit,
save or rejoin. A recipe restriction cannot replace the physical reason an
arrangement succeeds or fails.

The current small-press prototype accepts one fitted receiver tray in either
side cell perpendicular to the drive shaft. A powered stroke puts its one-unit
offcut there while the plate exits along the drive axis. The tray holds four
offcuts; a full or incompatible tray holds the next ready work until cleared.
Hand-cranked strokes retain manual offcut pickup at the anvil. This is an early
discharge choice, not the proposed sizing grate or a general gravity rule.

At minimum, the playable chapter should demonstrate two meaningfully different
arrangements for each machine, such as opposite-side drive or alternate feed
height. Present one legible example in staged Help, then let the player alter
it. An engineering challenge after the first product should invite a more
compact or more continuous line using familiar parts, with multiple valid
solutions. It should not require hidden construction order.

## Size-scaled copper forming and broad-sheet handling

The user-directed next engineering step keeps the hand-tended **1 × 1 press**
valid for the first plate and adds a **2 × 2 forming area** assembled from
repeated, independently placed workbed and working-face parts. Both use the
common world grid and one thickness. The four cells form one supported stroke for one broad
workpiece, rather than four adjacent independent presses. Matching contact
faces, powered-slide travel, contiguous workpiece support, clearance and a capable
drive determine whether forming can occur. The working area does not mandate
one complete actuator or shaft/cog pair per tile; actual drive capacity and
physical coupling determine the required motion components. An enlarged recipe
or gifted large machine does not substitute for those facts.
No technology lock prevents a player who has enough parts from trying the
larger layout early.

| Initial route | Exact mass custody in the prototype comparison | Distinct physical cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Four small strokes | Four stock chunks × 4 mass units = four plates × 3 plus four recoverable offcuts × 1. | Four work cycles and their feed, removal and offcut recovery. |
| One broad forming and shear route | Three stock chunks × 4 mass units = one 2 × 2 sheet of 12, then four plates × 3 with no offcut. | Higher peak drive, four-cell workpiece support and clearance, broad-sheet transfer to a cutter. |

The apparent three-quarter fresh-stock cost of the large route is **provisional
balance**, not a mass discount: four recovered small offcuts hold enough mass
for another plate if an explicit reprocessing operation accepts them. The
material ledger must follow stock, sheet, plate and offcut form through every
hand, tray, machine and carrier. A refusal, occupied output, dismantle, save or
two-client continuation must preserve both mass and partially completed work.
After tuning, the broad route must still offer a real material or sustained
throughput benefit; complete offcut recycling may erase its initial fresh-stock
advantage, so the larger machine also needs a useful production-rate payoff.
This replaces the current single-blank product fixture without invalidating
the fixture's historical evidence. “Copper” from an exposed native deposit is
a provisional name and geology choice; ordinary copper ore is not silently
cold-formable stock. A preparation step is required if the selected deposit
yields ore rather than workable metal.

A normal one-wide belt refuses the broad sheet by footprint. One candidate
uses a gantry carriage to move the workpiece between forming and cutting tools;
which tools ride with it remains a prototype choice. A clear gravity slide or
drop to a lower cutter offers another route. Both need visible swept volume,
support and backpressure. Any gantry carriage and attached tooling form one
internally moving stationary installation; this does not authorize mobile
factories or pushing independent machines. Blocked transfer leaves the broad
item accessible for repair and retry. Further sizes and exact ratios are open;
variants should come from geometry and conserved quantity rather than a recipe
per size.

Build and verify native mass, 1 × 1 and 2 × 2 assembly, broad-item carriage,
gantry and vertical transfer before making new Ponder lessons for this loop.
The lessons should show actual construction, material travel and recovery,
not cover missing mechanics. The implementation family is

## Material quality creates a choice

Part material sets transmitted load and suitable working conditions; geometry
still controls connection and direction. A wooden shaft or cog handles a small
early machine at ordinary speed. A heavier combined collector/press line
exceeds a wooden bottleneck but can run after replacing that bottleneck with
softmetal. Metal costs scarce blanks and more shaping, while wooden parts stay
useful for light branches and remote early builds. A later alloy can enable
still higher load or hostile-site operation through a genuinely new process.

For the first fixture, size the ratings so **one hand-cranked machine works
through wood, a combined powered line stalls at a wooden bottleneck, and the
same line runs through an equivalent metal part**. Exact torque, speed,
fatigue and load units must be tuned against the existing native solver before
being canonized. Stress above a part rating stalls the affected connected
drive without destroying parts or contents, matching the established
non-destructive obstruction rule. Couplings may isolate a weak branch. Clear
feedback names the limiting part and a likely remedy, without a wall of text.

Quality is not a random grade roll or a blanket “metal is better” tier. Each
material has a concrete affordance, cost and limit, and design choices can
reduce load, split branches or change ratios instead of always requiring an
upgrade. Wooden and metal parts use the same tile origin and connection rules,
including elevated foundations.

## Agency, multiplayer and evidence

The AI asks for a delivery, not a prescribed build. Orders can suggest a
collector and press, but manual acquisition, alternative layouts and early
exploration remain viable. The pod and hub provide a reliable foothold, not a
permanent base location. Any part needed to replace the hub must be obtainable
before dismantling it, and dismantling must return or preserve its nonconsumed
tooling. If an order consumes goods, its costs belong in the bootstrap budget.

The pod, tree/deposit reserves, hub buffers, carried items, placed components,
machine work and delivery stock have one native authority. Each operation is
actor-bound and retry-safe. A shared world gets one landing stock; personal
manager messages and learning pace remain separable from team/world ownership.
The old four-plate nonconsuming objective and complete-machine scene are
historical test fixtures until a new delivery contract is implemented.

The chapter task is complete only after a normal build can be played from
arrival through a first product, with a correct save/rejoin and two-client
stock path. Native tests can establish accounting and geometry; Godot checks
can establish rendering and control paths; screenshots can establish visible
details at a named revision. Only human play can establish whether the arrival,
part design and progression are understandable and enjoyable. The Bead subtree
`factory-yau.1.10` keeps those outcomes separate while owners integrate the
working game.
