# Independent design review

Date: 2026-10-14.

The reviewer was given DESIGN.md as its only project context, without the conversation history. This is a review of an early-stage design document, not an implementation specification. The findings below are recommendations for further discussion.

## Overall assessment

A coherent, distinctive discovery brief. Modular machinery, geometry-dependent scaling, gradual automation, and dependable operation reinforce one another. The document carefully distinguishes requirements from proposals and recognizes major technical risks. Its main weakness is that the intended experience is much more concrete than the smallest complete game that could deliver it.

## Prioritized findings

1. **Prove the central design hypothesis with one concrete production problem.** The reference examples explain the desired pleasure unusually well, but the progression slice still uses placeholder products. Specify a small tool vocabulary and an actual A/B/C relationship, then demonstrate two substantially different working arrangements with meaningful tradeoffs. Include a reason to build vertically that affects operation or scalability. This will reveal whether the rules support invention and whether verticality creates engineering choices or merely adds construction steps. Detailed recipes and a full catalogue can wait. References: [reference experiences](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:28), [progression relationship](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:179), [prototype slice](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:388).

2. **Establish a production boundary for the first complete release.** Required moving factories, trains, passengers, transfers during motion, multiplayer, multiple teams, terrain modification, fluids/gases, teaching demonstrations, and two campaign endings form an ambitious combined scope. That is particularly consequential alongside solo development, limited experience, and rapid iteration. The prototype proposal stages work sensibly, but does not define a smallest complete game or distinguish launch requirements from eventual requirements. Choose those boundaries before expanding the content plan. AI support does not remove the need to validate and integrate interacting systems. References: [construction requirements](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:50), [campaign and multiplayer](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:110), [development approach](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:129), [candidate prototype](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:384).

3. **Decide what independent progression means in a shared physical world.** The document rightly leaves individual/team/world ownership open. However, personal unlock tracks alone cannot preserve learning pace when other players can build advanced machinery, exhaust deposits, modify terrain, and advance revelations in the same world. Decide whether independent pace means separate instruction and credit, restricted access to capabilities, separate territory, or another arrangement. This affects world structure and campaign design, so it deserves resolution ahead of substantial content production. References: [progression ownership](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:119), [quest completion](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:279), [multiplayer architecture considerations](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:325).

4. **Define the incentives that make redesign worthwhile.** The preference for advanced methods over overclocking or duplication is clear, but players will encounter practical costs as well as intended satisfaction. A harder method needs advantages large enough to justify rebuilding, while alternative arrangements should remain useful. Select the main comparison axes—such as footprint, throughput, feedstock use, power, or construction effort—and test them in the first chapter. The throughput ending should reinforce those choices; otherwise large-scale duplication could become the easiest route through the culminating challenge. References: [scaling preferences](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:79), [method tradeoffs](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:187), [campaign closure and mastery](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:195).

5. **Make construction and diagnosis an early experience test.** The proposed loop depends on observing and diagnosing machinery, while camera/navigation, detailed feedback, and construction convenience remain open. Deferring their final form is reasonable, but a crowded vertical factory must still be understandable and comfortable to edit. Include a representative session of building, tracing a stalled drivetrain, correcting feeding, and expanding the machine in the first playable evaluation. Teaching demonstrations alone cannot establish that live machinery is legible. References: [play loop](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:209), [construction convenience](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:222), [collision and motion considerations](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:291), [deferred topics](/home/dan/factory_project/DESIGN.md:358).

## Most useful next decisions

Develop a concrete first chapter, establish the first-release boundary, and define progression ownership. Story specifics, exact endgame rates, and detailed collision exceptions can remain deferred while those decisions establish the game's shape.
